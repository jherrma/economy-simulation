# The opening balance sheet

**Epic:** E3 — Configuration and opening state
**Depends on:** 03-03, 02-03
**New ground:** A t = 0 state that satisfies V1 before a single tick runs

## Story

As the model author, I want the town's opening stocks constructed so that the base-money identity already holds, so that the run starts from a consistent state rather than converging toward one.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Household opening assets come from §13.11's `household_assets_0`, in months of that household's own income, tied to wealth decile.
- [ ] The split between cash and deposits at t = 0 follows each household's `κ`.
- [ ] Firms are initialised from the §5.2.1 per-sector ranges: `capital_stock`, `cash`, `deposits`, `initial_debt`.
- [ ] Bank reserves follow as `M0 −` public cash `−` firm cash. They are **derived**, never configured.
- [ ] Opening mortgages carry drawn `initial_ltv` and `initial_remaining_term`, so the town does not amortise in lockstep.
- [ ] Bank equity is the residual, and its sign and size are checked in 03-05.
- [ ] **V1 passes at t = 0**, before the first tick.

## Where to start

The ordering matters: reserves are what is left of `M0` after the public and firms hold their cash, so
they cannot be an input. Anything that configures reserves directly will break V1 at t = 0 or, worse, be
'fixed' by relaxing V1.

The drawn remaining terms are not decoration. Without them every mortgage in town matures in the same
month and the run develops a synchronised repayment wave that looks like a credit cycle and is an
artefact of initialisation. The §13.1 warm-up of 240 ticks exists partly to clear this; it cannot clear
something that is regenerated by construction.

Expect the opening loan book to be several times `M0`. That is not a bug — §13.1 works through why, and
time deposits are what fund it — but it is what 03-05 has to check.

## How to verify

```sh
dotnet test --filter FullyQualifiedName~OpeningStateTests
```

V1 green at t = 0 for every `reserve_ratio` in the sweep, including 1.00 and 0.01.
